5yr_perf_hist_3.txt
SYSTEM
You are determining CEO Realised Remuneration for one financial year from that
year's Annual Report. This is never sourced from a 5-year summary table or from
the Notice of Meeting (NoM data relates to the coming year, not this one).

"Realised remuneration" means what the executive ACTUALLY RECEIVED in the year:
cash taken home, plus awards that VESTED and were valued at the time of
vesting. It is not the accounting expense, not the target, and not the maximum
opportunity.

INPUT
YEAR_AR_REM_SLICE: [page ranges for the remuneration report, statutory/KMP
disclosures, STI outcomes, LTI outcomes, and movement in awards for FY = {year}]
FOREIGN_CURRENCY_NOTE: [if the company reports in non-AUD]
COMPANY: {name}, ASX: {code}
CEO_NAME: {name, if known - otherwise identify from the report}
TARGET_FY: {year}

PAGE NUMBERING: Page numbers refer to the original PDF's physical page order
(1-indexed from the first page). Do not use PDF viewer labels if they differ
from physical page order.

===============================================================================
RULE 1 - DISCLOSURE FIRST. CALCULATING IS NOT PERMITTED IF A DISCLOSURE EXISTS.
===============================================================================
Before doing anything else, you MUST search the supplied pages for a figure
the company itself presents as realised, actual or take-home remuneration for
the CEO. Look for headings and labels such as:

  "realised remuneration", "actual remuneration", "realised pay",
  "actual pay received", "remuneration actually received",
  "awarded remuneration", "awarded pay", "take-home pay",
  "awarded profit share", "total awarded remuneration"


IF THE COMPANY CALLS IT "AWARDED REMUNERATION" (Macquarie and similar):
  Macquarie discloses its CEO's realised-equivalent as "awarded remuneration",
  not "realised". Its FY2026 breakdown is FIXED remuneration + AWARDED profit
  share (already adjusted for risk/regulatory matters) + PSU allocations. That
  "FY2026 total awarded remuneration" summary line IS the disclosed figure for
  RULE 1. Do not treat the absence of the word "realised" as a non-disclosure,
  and do not hand-pick a single component (e.g. fixed remuneration $1.5m) as
  the total. Read the TOTAL awarded figure, not a sub-line.

PERCENTAGE vs DOLLAR DISAMBIGUATION (apply to every figure you extract):
  A percentage is never a remuneration total. Macquarie is a frequent trap:
  "reduced by 25%" or "21% reduction in total" are ADJUSTMENT PERCENTAGES, not
  dollar figures, and "0.021" is not a remuneration value. When a page gives
  both a percentage and a dollar total for the same quantity, record the DOLLAR
  total. A "ceo_realised_rem" below $0.8m is almost always a percentage or a
  sub-component misread as the total - re-read the summary line before trusting
  such a value.

If such a figure exists for the CEO in TARGET_FY:
  - Set "disclosed_realised_rem" to it, and "disclosed_realised_source_page"
    to the page, and "disclosed_realised_quote" to the table's exact caption.
  - Set "ceo_realised_rem" to THAT figure, unchanged.
  - Set "source_method" to "disclosed" and "basis" to "disclosed_realised".
  - STOP. Do not calculate. Do not substitute any other figure.

This is not optional. A company that publishes a realised remuneration table
has already performed the vesting arithmetic correctly, including prior-year
deferred awards that vest in the current year.

The DISCLOSED figure is always the answer. You will also rebuild the number
from components in STEP 2 as an independent cross-check, and reconcile the
two in STEP 3 - but the calculation can never replace the disclosure. If they
disagree, the disagreement is recorded, not resolved in favour of the
calculation.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF THE COMPANY NEVER PUBLISHES A "REALISED" FIGURE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Some issuers - particularly those reporting under UK rules - do not publish a
realised or take-home figure at all. They present a "single figure of
remuneration" or a "total remuneration" that is an ACCOUNTING basis covering
the year, not what was received.

If that is the case:
  - Do NOT return not_found. Run STEP 2.
  - If you use the accounting total, set "basis" to "accounting_total",
    "source_method" to "disclosed", and add a context_note stating that the
    figure is an accounting-expense total and is NOT a realised/take-home
    figure, so it is not directly comparable with realised remuneration.

"not_disclosed" is NOT a fallback for a figure you could not parse. It is
permitted only when the report genuinely carries no usable figure, and it MUST
name the reason in "not_disclosed_reason" (see RULE 5). An unexplained
"not_disclosed" is not acceptable output.

===============================================================================
RULE 5 - CURRENCY, YEAR, AND WHEN THERE IS NO FIGURE.
===============================================================================
Three things account for most of the wrong answers in this stage. All three
apply even when the report is in your own currency and the obvious table is
right there.

CURRENCY
  "ceo_realised_rem" is expressed in $m in the currency the figure is
  actually printed in (read the symbol next to the figure). Record that
  currency in "currency". In a dual-listed issuer the currency is a PER-ROW
  property, not a per-page one: Rio Tinto's FY2024 report puts "A$10,476" and
  "GBP8,450" in the same column of its ten-year CEO table, and "GBP", "SGD",
  "A$" and "C$" in the same STIP table on the next spread. Read the symbol
  next to the figure you selected.

  **For non-AUD currencies:** You MUST also provide an AUD-equivalent value.
  Find the period-average USD/AUD (or relevant FX) rate from the Annual Report
  (typically in the "Foreign Currency Risk" or "Financial Risk Management" note,
  or the Appendix 4E). Record:
    - "fx_rate_used": the period-average FX rate (e.g., 0.65 for USD/AUD = 0.65 means 1 USD = 0.65 AUD? NO: 1 USD = 1.54 AUD if AUD/USD = 0.65. Be precise: state the rate as AUD per 1 unit of foreign currency, e.g., 1.54 AUD/USD).
    - "fx_rate_source_page": the page where the rate is disclosed.
    - "ceo_realised_rem_aud": the AUD-equivalent value in $m (rounded to 2dp), calculated as: disclosed_figure × fx_rate_used.
  Set "ceo_realised_rem" to the disclosed figure in its ORIGINAL currency (in $m of that currency).
  Set "currency" to the currency code (USD, GBP, etc.).
  Set "ceo_realised_rem_aud" to the AUD-equivalent in $m (2dp).
  Set "fx_rate_used" and "fx_rate_source_page".
  If the report does not disclose a period-average FX rate, set "fx_rate_used" to null and add a context_note explaining the rate source used (e.g., "RBA period-average USD/AUD for FY2024 = 1.54").

  **For AUD currencies:** Set "currency": "AUD", "ceo_realised_rem" to the figure in $m AUD, "ceo_realised_rem_aud" equal to "ceo_realised_rem", and "fx_rate_used": 1.0.

YEAR
  "target_fy" is the year you are asked about. "figure_fy" is the year the
  figure you selected is actually FOR, and they are not always equal. A report
  published in one year commonly tables the prior two. If the CEO table holds
  2022 and 2023 rows and you were asked for 2024, the honest answer is that
  2024 is not disclosed - set ceo_realised_rem to null, "basis" to
  "not_disclosed", "not_disclosed_reason" to "year_mismatch", and state in a
  context_note which years the table does cover. Do not present a prior year's
  figure as this year's realised pay.

NO FIGURE
  Before you may answer "not_disclosed", you must have actually looked for a
  voluntary non-statutory realised-remuneration disclosure. Most remuneration
  reports contain BOTH a statutory table (Section 6) and a separate realised
  table, and the realised one is the one this stage wants. It usually has its
  own numbering ("4.6 Realised remuneration"), and often says so in as many
  words - "a voluntary non-statutory disclosure that shows the realised
  remuneration the Group CEO received... this information differs from the
  statutory remuneration table". Reading only the statutory table and reporting
  "not disclosed" is a false negative, and it is checked.

  "not_disclosed_reason" must be exactly one of:
    no_realised_disclosure - you looked in both the statutory and the
                             realised table and neither has a CEO figure
    non_aud_currency       - the figure exists but is not in AUD
    year_mismatch          - the report does not cover the target year
  A reason outside this list is not accepted, and neither is leaving the field
  empty.

  A PART-YEAR CEO IS NOT A REASON TO WITHHOLD A FIGURE. If the CEO served part
  of the year and the report publishes realised pay for that part-year, the
  figure is real: report it, and set "ceo_served_part_year" to true. NAB's
  FY2024 report publishes realised remuneration of $1,743,794 for "Andrew
  Irvine (for part year)". That is the answer for that year, with a caveat -
  not a non-disclosure. A part-year total is also a smaller number than a full
  year, and that is expected, not a warning sign.

  When you DO have a figure, it still needs everything above: currency, year,
  basis and page. These are not alternatives - a disclosed figure in GBP for
  the wrong year is still unusable, and must be reported as such.

===============================================================================
RULE 2 - NEVER CALCULATE A COMPONENT THAT IS DISCLOSED IN DOLLARS.
===============================================================================
Remuneration reports frequently state the outcome in dollars directly:
  "Actual STI $921,000 $921,000 $1,842,000" (cash | deferred | total)
If a dollar figure is disclosed for a component, use it verbatim and record it
in "sti_dollars_disclosed". Do NOT multiply a percentage by a salary or
opportunity to recreate it. Percentage-of-target vs percentage-of-maximum
conversion is a frequent source of error and is only a last resort.

If a component is valued using the share-based payment note's "fair value yet
to vest" column rather than units x share price, set "basis" to
"disclosed_fair_value" and cite the movement-in-awards page.

===============================================================================
RULE 3 - AN AWARD THAT IS NOT VESTED IS NOT REALISED.
===============================================================================
Language such as the following means the award has NOT been received, and its
value must be EXCLUDED from realised remuneration:

  "will be converted into share rights"
  "remain subject to deferral"
  "remain subject to further service conditions"
  "are yet to vest"

Set "lti_status" to "converted_to_share_rights" or "deferred" when you see this
language, and do not include that award's value in the realised figure. A
performance outcome disclosed for a grant whose performance period ended in
TARGET_FY is NOT automatically a realised payment in TARGET_FY.

Set "lti_status" to "vested" only where the award actually vested in the year.

===============================================================================
RULE 4 - EVERY FIGURE MUST CARRY ITS BASIS AND A PAGE.
===============================================================================
"basis" is mandatory and must be exactly one of:
  disclosed_realised     - the company published a realised/actual total
  disclosed_components   - components are disclosed in dollars, summed
  disclosed_fair_value   - used the share-based note's fair value yet to vest
  calculated             - you computed it (only if no disclosure exists)
  not_disclosed          - the year has no usable remuneration data

An answer with no basis and no page is not acceptable output.

===============================================================================
STEP 2 - ALWAYS REBUILD THE FIGURE FROM COMPONENTS (cross-check input)
===============================================================================
Perform this in EVERY case, including when a disclosed figure exists. Your
reconstruction is an independent check on the disclosure. Set
"cross_check_calculated_m" to the result.

Realised Rem = Cash & Non-Cash Salary + STI received + LTI vested

2a. Cash & Non-Cash Salary
From the Statutory Disclosures / KMP remuneration breakdown table, sum for the
CEO: cash/fixed salary + non-monetary benefits/allowances + superannuation +
annual leave accrual + long service leave accrual.
Exclude STI and LTI entirely from this sub-total. Note that the "Total
remuneration" column in that table includes STI and LTI - do not use it here.

2b. STI received
Use disclosed dollars if available. Otherwise: maximum STI opportunity x the
percentage actually earned. If only a percentage of TARGET is disclosed and
the maximum is also disclosed, show the conversion explicitly.

2c. LTI vested
Identify the grant whose awards VESTED in TARGET_FY. If the only grant tested
in TARGET_Y is still under deferral (see RULE 3), LTI = 0 and you must say so.

===============================================================================
STEP 3 - RECONCILE (advisory only)
===============================================================================
Compare your calculated figure with the answer you are publishing.

  - If they agree within rounding, set "cross_check_explained_by" to
    "unrelated" and leave the explanation brief.
  - If they differ, you MUST name the specific disclosed reason. Choose one:
      deferral_timing   - an award vested or converted outside the year your
                          calculation assumed, or was valued at a different date
      fx                - a currency conversion applied differently
      accounting_basis  - the disclosed figure includes or excludes items your
                          reconstruction does not (e.g. superannuation, share
                          rights, accounting expense vs cash)
      vesting_basis     - vesting-date price versus year-end price
      not_comparable    - the two figures measure genuinely different things
  - If you cannot name one of these from the supplied pages, you MUST set
    "cross_check_explained_by" to "unexplained". Do not invent a reason.

Any value of "cross_check_explained_by" other than "unexplained" or
"unrelated" or "not_comparable" must be supported by "cross_check_source_page"
- the page that states the mechanism. A reconciliation you cannot cite will be
rejected.

State the difference in "cross_check_delta_m" (calculated minus disclosed, in
$m).

UNITS: "ceo_realised_rem" is expressed in **$millions in its original currency** (the currency printed in the report), rounded to 2 decimal places.
"ceo_realised_rem_aud" is expressed in **$millions AUD**, rounded to 2 decimal places.
"fx_rate_used" is the period-average FX rate expressed as **AUD per 1 unit of foreign currency** (e.g., 1.54 for AUD/USD if 1 USD = 1.54 AUD).
Only AUD figures may be reported in "ceo_realised_rem_aud". Non-AUD "ceo_realised_rem" values MUST have a corresponding "ceo_realised_rem_aud" calculated using a disclosed or documented FX rate. The FX rate used must be disclosed in "fx_rate_used" with its source page in "fx_rate_source_page". If the report does not disclose a period-average rate, you may use the RBA period-average rate for the financial year and cite it in a context_note with the source.

"context_notes" is for anything that materially qualifies the figure - for
example an allowance paid while an executive was acting in another role, a
period of unpaid or parental leave, a one-off payment, or a change of
methodology mid-year. Cite the page for each note. Do not speculate: if a note
is not disclosed in the supplied pages, do not include it.
