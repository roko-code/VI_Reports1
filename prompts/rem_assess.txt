Each entry in the news block includes a headline, a short snippet, a source, a publication date and a URL. Before using any entry:

IDENTIFYING THE SUBJECT MATTER

Before starting the guideline-by-guideline assessment, extract and state:
- Company name and financial year covered by the Annual Report.
- The name(s) and title(s) of the CEO (and Managing Director, if combined) for the year being assessed. If there was a CEO transition during the year, name both and state the transition date.
- The page range (see "Citing evidence" below) of the remuneration report within the Annual Report.
- Every remuneration-related resolution in the Notice of Meeting: its number, its exact title/short description as worded in the NoM, and which Section 5 sub-type (if any) it corresponds to. If a resolution doesn't map to anything in this prompt's scope (e.g. it's a director election), note that it's out of scope and move on.

Notices of Meeting typically number resolutions sequentially through a "Business of the Meeting" section (director elections are usually numbered alongside remuneration resolutions, not separated out — do not assume remuneration resolutions are grouped together or numbered distinctly). Each resolution is usually followed, later in the document, by its own "Explanatory Notes" entry giving the detail needed for assessment (e.g. LTI grant terms, termination benefit scope) — use the explanatory notes as the primary evidence source for that resolution, not just the short resolution wording itself. The remuneration report resolution's explanatory notes will often themselves state the printed page range of the remuneration report within the annual report — extract and use that if present, as a check on your own page identification.

Two resolution patterns are easy to mis-map — watch for them specifically:
- A "grant of performance rights" / "equity grant" resolution for a specific named executive is the CEO/MD equity grant sub-type, even if its title doesn't use the word "remuneration." Cross-check its stated hurdles, weightings and vesting schedule directly against the standing LTI Plan terms disclosed in the remuneration report for the same company — flag any difference (e.g. a changed comparator-company list, a changed performance threshold) as something requiring explanation, since a new grant's terms should normally be consistent with the plan as described in the remuneration report unless the company explains a change.
- A "prospective" or "refresh" termination-benefits resolution (seeking approval under Corporations Act s200B/200E for future, unquantified termination payments to any current or future KMP) is a distinct pattern from a one-off resolution approving a specific, quantified termination payment to a named individual. Both fall under the CEO/Non-CEO termination benefit sub-types, but a prospective/blanket approval cannot be assessed against "quantum" in the way the guideline test describes, because no quantum is yet known. For this pattern, instead assess: whether the approval merely preserves existing STI/LTI plan rules and discretions (rather than expanding them), whether the underlying plan rules it relies on already satisfy REM-S13 (malus/clawback) and REM-S09 (discretion is justified and consistent with shareholder experience), how recently the same approval was last sought (a very long gap, or no time limit at all on the previous approval, is itself worth noting), and whether the explanatory notes disclose the factors that would determine value if the discretion were exercised. State clearly in your evidence/action text that this is a prospective approval, not a quantified payment, so the human reviewer isn't looking for a dollar figure that doesn't exist yet.


CITING EVIDENCE

Every result you report must be traceable to a specific page. Cite the printed page number — i.e., the page number shown in the document's own header or footer, the number a human would use to navigate the physical or PDF document — NOT a raw PDF page index/count, since that's not what a reviewer sees when they open the document and look for the page number printed on it. Notices of Meeting themselves use this convention (e.g. this company's own NoM cites its remuneration report as "pages 58 to 80" of the annual report, meaning the printed page numbers) — match that convention so your citations are directly usable without translation.

Format citations as: [p.N] or [p.N–M] for a range. When a figure is quoted, cite the exact page it came from, not the page the surrounding section starts on. If a document has no visible printed page number on a given page (e.g. a cover page), cite the nearest preceding printed page number and note "unnumbered page following p.N".

If you cannot find supporting evidence for something the guideline requires, do not cite a page "close enough" — say the evidence was not found, and result Uncertain.


DISCLOSURE vs INTERPRETATION

For every row, keep two things visibly separate in your evidence/comparison text:
- What the company disclosed (a fact, a figure, a stated policy) — state this plainly and cite it.
- ASA's judgement about whether that disclosure meets the guideline — this is your assessment, and should read as an assessment, not be blended into the fact.

Example of what NOT to do: "The company's peer group is inadequate [PDF p.64]." (blends fact and judgement)
Example of what TO do: "The company discloses a TIC-industry peer group of 6 named companies for the EBITDA margin hurdle [PDF p.71]. This is a reasonably sized, named, industry-relevant comparator group, which satisfies the disclosure standard in REM-M01."


CONFIDENCE

For every row you complete (guideline assessment or calculation), assign a confidence level:
- High (95–100%): the evidence is unambiguous, directly stated, and complete.
- Medium (80–94%): the evidence supports your result but required some interpretation, reconciliation across tables, or minor extrapolation.
- Low (below 80%): the evidence is incomplete, contradictory, presented only in a chart/graphic without an accompanying data table, or you had to make a material assumption.

If your confidence is Medium or Low, state briefly why in the evidence/action column.


HANDLING MISSING OR INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

- Set the result to Uncertain (U). Do not mark Pass or Fail by default, and do not mark N/A unless the guideline is genuinely not applicable to this company's structure (e.g. REM-S12 sign-on awards when no sign-on award was made this year — that IS a legitimate N/A).
- State explicitly, in the evidence/action field, what evidence is missing and what would be needed to complete the assessment.
- Do not lower your stated confidence to compensate for a missing-evidence Uncertain result — confidence describes how sure you are given what you found, not a proxy for completeness. A confidently-reported Uncertain (e.g. "High confidence that this cannot be assessed without the prior year report") is fine and preferable to a hedged guess.


NUMERIC EXTRACTION — GUARDRAILS

Remuneration reports typically contain more than one remuneration figure for the same person, defined differently (e.g. statutory/accounting-expense remuneration under AASB 124 vs a company's own "actual/received" or "realised" non-IFRS table). These are NOT interchangeable, and using the wrong one will produce a wrong assessment — most importantly for REM-M02 (pay–performance alignment) and the "realised remuneration" row in Section 4, both of which require the actual/take-home figure, not the statutory accounting expense.

Before performing any calculation:
1. Quote the exact source table name and the exact figure(s) you are using, with page citations.
2. State which remuneration basis the figure represents (statutory/accounting expense, vs actual/received/realised, vs target, vs maximum opportunity) and confirm it's the correct basis for this particular guideline or calculation.
3. Show your calculation as a visible step (e.g. "$1,610,194 cash STI + $690,083 deferred equity STI = $2,300,276 total STI awarded [PDF p.68]"), not just the final number.

Prefer figures from a data table over figures read off a chart, graph, or donut/pie chart image. If a required figure is only available from a chart (no accompanying table), you may use it, but cap your confidence at Medium and note this.

If a document mixes multiple currencies (e.g. an executive's pay partly earned outside Australia and converted to AUD by the company), use the company's own converted/reported figures and note the conversion basis the company discloses, rather than re-converting yourself.


USING THE COMPARATOR CSV (if supplied)

If a CEO/KMP remuneration comparator dataset is supplied, it will contain one row per executive per financial year, across a universe of companies, with (at minimum) these fields: Company Name, Csuite Job Title, Financial Year End, Currency Future Rem, Total Fixed Remuneration (TFR), Target STI, Max STI, LTI Performance Target, LTI Performance Max, Restricted Shares (LTI Time), Salary Maximum, Market Cap, ASX index membership flags, Primary Exchange, Sector.

Use it only for the peer-quantum comparisons required by REM-M01 and the "Peer quantum" row in Section 4. Rules for using it:

1. Filter to rows where Csuite Job Title is "Chief Executive Officer" and the row is the most recent available financial year for that company (an "Internal"/"Latest" indicator or similar may be present — use the latest-year row per company).
2. Compute two figures for each peer, using the company's own disclosed field values:
   - Target Remuneration = Total Fixed Remuneration (TFR) + Target STI + LTI Performance Target (excludes Restricted Shares).
   - Salary Maximum = Total Fixed Remuneration (TFR) + Max STI + LTI Performance Max + Restricted Shares (LTI Time) + Retention bonus. (This matches the dataset's own "Salary Maximum" column where present — use that column directly if it's populated, rather than recomputing, and only recompute if it's blank.)
3. Compute the equivalent two figures for the subject company's own CEO, using the same formula, from the figures you extracted from the Annual Report (not from the CSV, even if the subject company happens to appear in it — extracted evidence takes priority for the company under assessment).
4. Currency: the CSV has no FX conversion built in. Every peer row is denominated in the currency stated in its own "Currency Future Rem" field. To compare peers to the subject company, convert all figures to a single common currency (AUD, unless the subject company reports in a different currency) using an approximate current spot rate. State the exchange rate(s) you used and the approximate date they apply to — precision is not required, this is for magnitude comparison only, not for any figure that will itself be reported as a disclosed fact.
5. Peer selection: prefer the peer group the company itself names in its own disclosure (e.g. a named TIC-industry comparator group, or a named ASX-index comparator group for TSR) over selecting your own peers from the CSV. Only build your own peer set from the CSV when the company's own disclosed peer group is inadequate or missing — in that case, select comparators by matching sector/industry classification and market capitalisation (roughly 0.5x–2x the subject company's market cap) and same primary exchange where possible, and state explicitly that this is an ASA-constructed comparator set, not a company-disclosed one, and why.
6. Report the subject company's percentile/quartile rank within whichever peer set was used, and state which peer set (company-disclosed or ASA-constructed) it is.

SECTION 2 — CEO REMUNERATION MAJOR GUIDELINES

A major guideline failure normally supports Against on the relevant remuneration resolution unless convincing documented mitigation applies. Assess each of the following:

REM-M01 — No overpayment.
Test: CEO remuneration must not be excessive relative to appropriate peers, scale, complexity, geography and required capability. Review disclosed benchmarking, peer selection rationale, named custom peers, sample sufficiency, quartile rank and independent comparators where needed. Missing custom-peer companies or quartile rank is a significant disclosure failure — do not infer a Pass in that case.
Vote linkage: Major; normally Against if failed without convincing mitigation.

REM-M02 — Pay–performance alignment.
Test: Realised CEO remuneration must align with shareholder value creation and financial/cultural outcomes over at least three years (longer periods preferred). Compare reconciled realised/take-home pay (not statutory accounting expense) with TSR, dividends, earnings/returns, material losses, risk and conduct outcomes. Poor outcomes should reduce rewards; strong results do not cure poor scheme design.
Vote linkage: Major; normally Against if failed without convincing mitigation.

REM-M03 — Prior issues resolved.
Test: Material remuneration concerns raised in prior years must be adequately addressed. Use the prior ASA Voting Intentions/AGM report plus the prior-year remuneration report. Track each concern, the company's commitment, any structural change made, and the outcome. Repeated unresolved secondary issues may become decisive.
Vote linkage: Major; normally Against if failed without convincing mitigation.
(If prior year documents were not supplied, mark Uncertain per the missing-evidence rule above.)

REM-M04 — Face-value equity grants.
Test: The number of equity awards must be determined using face value / current market price at grant — normally VWAP — not fair-value discounting. Separate the grant-number methodology from the accounting fair value. Confirm the VWAP period, price, award number and any dividend adjustment used. Accounting requirements do not justify fair-value allocation.
Vote linkage: Major; normally Against the remuneration report and the relevant grant if failed.

REM-M05 — No option-based incentives.
Test: Incentives must not rely on options or option-like instruments. Identify any leverage, strike price, settlement mechanism, cash-equivalent features, and asymmetric upside. An early-stage cash-preservation exception requires credible justification and an appropriately stretching exercise price.
Vote linkage: Major; normally Against if failed and no genuine early-stage exception applies.


SECTION 3 — CEO REMUNERATION SECONDARY GUIDELINES

One secondary concern is not automatically decisive on its own. Assess materiality, repetition, transparency, quantum, alignment and accountability together across all 13. Assess each of the following:

REM-S01 — STI metrics. A majority of STI metrics are financial and objectively measurable; non-financial measures are quantified or have defined milestones; each metric and its weighting is disclosed.
REM-S02 — STI targets. Targets are challenging and their derivation references prior performance/outcomes. Above-target STI should require a specific financial gateway.
REM-S03 — STI deferral. At least 50% of STI is paid in equity that cannot be transacted for at least 12 months.
REM-S04 — LTI form. LTI is an equity award, not cash.
REM-S05 — LTI period. Minimum three-year performance period; prefer at least four years for capital-intensive, long-investment or stable long-term businesses.
REM-S06 — LTI measures. At least two appropriate metrics; financial metrics are the majority; LTI metrics differ from STI metrics. Relative-TSR comparator selection is justified and custom comparator companies are disclosed in a sufficiently large group.
REM-S07 — LTI targets. Targets are challenging and explained by reference to prior performance/outcomes.
REM-S08 — Hurdle integrity. No retesting, no retrospective changes to prior-year hurdles, and protection against windfall gains.
REM-S09 — Changes and discretion. Any remuneration change and any exercise of board discretion is clearly and reasonably justified and consistent with shareholder experience.
REM-S10 — KMP alignment. The company has a material shareholding requirement or equity plan giving the CEO and KMP significant skin in the game.
REM-S11 — Pay genuinely at risk. At least 60% of the CEO's total potential pay is genuinely at risk: fixed remuneration is less than the combined maximum STI and LTI opportunity.
REM-S12 — Sign-on awards. Any sign-on award is equity (not cash), not excessive, linked to genuine foregone incentives, and normally vests over three to five years subject to satisfactory performance. (Mark N/A if no sign-on award was made this year.)
REM-S13 — Malus and clawback. Enforceable malus and clawback provisions continue post-employment.


SECTION 4 — REQUIRED REMUNERATION CALCULATIONS AND COMPARISONS

Perform each of the following, showing your working and citations as described above:

1. What changed from last year — compare the current and prior remuneration reports (not just the prior ASA VI report) across: fixed pay, target/max STI and LTI, metrics, weightings, targets, deferral, vesting period, allocation method, discretion, shareholding policy, malus/clawback, and outcomes. (Requires prior year Annual Report; mark Uncertain if not supplied.)
2. Target and maximum framework — calculate fixed remuneration, STI cash, STI equity and LTI, in dollars and as percentages of total. State whether the company's own disclosed figures are target or maximum, and reconcile to the company's own definitions.
3. At-risk percentage — (maximum STI + maximum LTI) ÷ maximum total potential pay. Do not count fixed pay as at risk. State how you treated any non-standard components (e.g. retention payments, restricted shares).
4. STI equity percentage — equity-settled/locked STI ÷ total STI awarded. Confirm the minimum lock period from the award date or vesting date, as disclosed.
5. Realised remuneration — use the company's own disclosed actual/take-home/realised pay table only, after reconciling its definition. Distinguish vesting value, exercise proceeds and accounting expense; do not substitute the statutory total for this.
6. Pay–performance period — use a minimum of three complete years, preferably longer, with consistent dates for pay, share price and dividends. Explain any CEO transition or methodology break that affects comparability.
7. Peer quantum — test peer relevance, sample size, company scale and quartile position (see "Using the comparator CSV" above if a comparator dataset was supplied). Where company evidence is inadequate, perform an independent assessment (using the CSV, if available) or classify the row Uncertain.
